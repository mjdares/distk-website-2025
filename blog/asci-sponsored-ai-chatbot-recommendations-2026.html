'Sponsored by [Brand]': ASCI's 2026 Rule for Paid AI Recommendations

When an AI assistant recommends your product because you paid for it, ASCI now says the answer must carry three specific words. Here is the clause, how it differs from every other AI label in the guideline, and what it leaves unsaid.

Distk Editorial Oct 2026 10 min read

ASCI's Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising, signed on 17 September 2026 and released on 29 September 2026, list paid AI product suggestions under Mandatory Labelling Required. Clause 2F covers "Using AI for paid or sponsored product suggestions, which must specifically be labelled as 'sponsored by'", and clause 4 states the disclosure "should clearly state 'Sponsored by [Brand].'" ASCI's illustration is a chatbot asked about a moisturiser for Mumbai weather that recommends a brand because it paid for the recommendation. Unlike the other AI labels in the guideline, where brands may choose accurate wording, this one is specified. The guideline does not say where the label must sit, whether brand or platform applies it, or set separate penalties. It takes effect three months from publication.

What Does ASCI Say About Sponsored AI Chatbot Recommendations in 2026?

ASCI's 2026 guideline on synthetically generated content lists paid AI product suggestions as a case where labelling is mandatory. Clause 2F covers "Using AI for paid or sponsored product suggestions, which must specifically be labelled as 'sponsored by'." Clause 4 then fixes the wording: "Where AI recommends a product that is sponsored, the disclosure should clearly state 'Sponsored by [Brand].'" It is the first Indian advertising code to address paid answers inside AI assistants directly.

The guideline illustrates the rule with a scenario every marketer will recognise. A consumer asks an AI chatbot, "What is a good moisturiser for Mumbai weather?" The AI recommends a specific brand or product because the brand has paid for or sponsored that recommendation. In ASCI's words, "the recommendation should be clearly identified as 'Sponsored by [Brand]', so that the consumer understands that the recommendation is commercially influenced."

Why Does This Rule Matter More Than It First Appears in 2026?

Because a recommendation inside a conversation looks like advice, not like an advertisement. A banner or a sponsored search result has a familiar visual grammar that tells people it is paid. An answer from an assistant arrives in the same voice as every other answer, which is exactly why ASCI singled it out and why it specified a label rather than leaving the wording to the advertiser.

For Indian brands the timing is direct. Paid placements inside AI assistants are now a real buying option, as our ChatGPT Ads API guide and the overview of advertising inside AI in India set out. Any brand planning to buy those placements for Indian consumers now has an ASCI code requirement to account for alongside whatever the platform itself does.

  • The label wording is fixed. Elsewhere in the guideline brands may choose their own label. Here ASCI names the words.
  • The illustration is consumer advice. A moisturiser question is the kind of everyday query where a paid answer would be mistaken for a neutral one.
  • It applies to the recommendation itself. The clause is about the AI's product suggestion, not only about a separate ad unit next to it.

How Does the Sponsored Label Differ From Other AI Labels in 2026?

Most of ASCI's 2026 guideline lets brands pick appropriate wording. Clause 4 says brands "may use appropriate labels, either those provided by ad platforms or their own labels such as but not limited to 'Audio/Video created using AI' or 'Audio/Video enhanced using AI.'" It adds that "Brands may use alternative labels that accurately inform the consumer." The sponsored-recommendation case is treated differently: the disclosure "should clearly state 'Sponsored by [Brand].'"

The two labels disclose different things. An "Audio/Video created using AI" label tells a consumer how content was made. A "Sponsored by [Brand]" label tells them why they are seeing a recommendation. A sponsored AI recommendation that also uses AI-generated video could plausibly need both, because each answers a separate question the consumer would otherwise get wrong. The guideline does not address combined labels explicitly, so treat that as a judgement to confirm, not a rule.

What Does the Guideline Not Say About Sponsored AI Answers in 2026?

The clause is short, and several practical questions sit outside it. Planning around them honestly is better than assuming an answer ASCI has not given.

  • Where the label must appear in the interface, its size, or its placement relative to the answer. The guideline says the disclosure should "clearly state" the wording, and that disclaimers "should follow the ASCI Code on disclaimer guidelines where applicable."
  • Who applies the label, the brand or the platform. Clause 4 allows "labels provided by ad platforms", but the guideline does not allocate responsibility between them.
  • Any threshold of payment or relationship below which the label is not needed. The clause says "paid or sponsored".
  • Enforcement or penalties. The guideline states that "Advertisements containing SGC are subject to compliance with the entire ASCI Code." It does not set out separate penalties.

When Do the ASCI AI Labelling Guidelines Take Effect in 2026?

The guideline text says: "These Guidelines shall come into effect on the expiration of 3 months from the date of their publication." The document is signed by the Chairman of ASCI's Board of Governors and dated 17 September 2026. ASCI's press release, datelined Mumbai, 29 September 2026, says ASCI "today released" the guidelines and that they "will come into effect three months from the date of publication."

ASCI does not itself state the calendar date on which the guidelines take effect. Two dates appear in its documents, and they do different jobs. The 17 September 2026 date is when the guideline was signed. The 29 September 2026 date is when ASCI says it released it. If 29 September is treated as the date of publication, three months runs to 29 December 2026. Plan to that date at the latest, and confirm the effective date with ASCI before relying on it in a contract or compliance calendar.

How Should Brands Buying AI Ads Prepare in 2026?

Treat the sponsored label as a contractual and creative requirement now, rather than as a platform feature you will check later. The practical work is making sure that whatever surface carries your paid AI recommendations to Indian consumers can show the ASCI wording, and that someone on your side has confirmed it does.

  1. List every AI surface you pay for that could recommend a product to an Indian consumer, including tests and pilots.
  2. Check how each one labels paid placements today, and whether that label reads as "Sponsored by [Brand]" or something weaker.
  3. Put the label requirement in your insertion orders or platform briefs so the obligation is written down, not assumed.
  4. Brief your agency and creative team that this is a mandatory case with fixed wording, not one of the flexible labels.
  5. Keep the rest of the ASCI Code in view. A labelled sponsored answer still has to be truthful. The label does not cure a misleading claim.
The point to hold onto in 2026

ASCI is explicit elsewhere in the same guideline that some content "will violate the ASCI code even if an AI label is used." A sponsored recommendation for a product that cannot deliver what the answer implies is still a misleading advertisement. The label tells consumers who paid; it does not make an untrue recommendation acceptable.

What Are the Common Mistakes With Sponsored AI Recommendations in 2026?

  • Using a softer label. "Promoted" or "partner" may not meet a clause that names "Sponsored by [Brand]".
  • Assuming the platform handles it. The guideline allows platform labels but does not say the platform carries the obligation.
  • Treating it as best practice. It is listed under Mandatory Labelling Required.
  • Confusing the two label types. "Created using AI" and "Sponsored by [Brand]" disclose different facts.
  • Assuming the effective date is the signing date. The guideline was signed on 17 September and released on 29 September 2026; the three months run from publication.
  • Relying on the label to fix a weak claim. The whole ASCI Code still applies.

Key Takeaways for 2026

  • ASCI's 2026 SGC guideline makes labelling mandatory for "paid or sponsored product suggestions" made by AI.
  • The required wording is fixed: "Sponsored by [Brand]".
  • ASCI's illustration is a chatbot recommending a moisturiser for Mumbai weather because the brand paid for it.
  • Other AI labels are flexible; this one is not.
  • The guideline does not say where the label goes, who applies it, or set separate penalties.
  • ASCI released the guideline on 29 September 2026; it takes effect three months from publication, which is 29 December 2026 if that release date is the publication date. Confirm with ASCI.
  • A sponsored label does not excuse a misleading recommendation. The full ASCI Code applies.

Key Questions

Sponsored AI Recommendations: FAQs

What does ASCI require for sponsored AI chatbot recommendations?

Clause 2F of ASCI's 2026 SGC guideline makes labelling mandatory when AI is used for paid or sponsored product suggestions, and clause 4 says the disclosure should clearly state "Sponsored by [Brand]."

Can a brand use its own wording instead of 'Sponsored by [Brand]'?

For most AI labels in the guideline, yes: brands may use platform labels or alternatives that accurately inform the consumer. For sponsored AI recommendations, ASCI specifies the wording "Sponsored by [Brand]."

Does the label have to be applied by the brand or the platform?

The guideline does not allocate this. Clause 4 allows labels provided by ad platforms, but it does not say which party carries the obligation, so put the requirement in writing with any platform you buy from.

Does a 'Sponsored by' label make any recommendation acceptable?

No. ASCI states that some content will violate the ASCI Code even if an AI label is used, and that advertisements containing SGC are subject to the entire ASCI Code.

When does the sponsored AI rule take effect?

The guideline says it comes into effect three months from its publication. ASCI released it on 29 September 2026, so that would be 29 December 2026 if the release date is the publication date. ASCI does not state the calendar date itself.

Does this apply to ChatGPT ads shown in India?

The guideline applies to advertising in India covered by the ASCI Code. A paid product recommendation inside any AI assistant shown to Indian consumers falls within the clause's description.
