Google's February 2026 Discover Core Update: What Changed for Publishers

Discover-specific core updates are rare, and this one came with unusually plain guidance about what Google wants. Here is what it rewards, what it penalises, and what it means once it reaches Indian readers.

Distk Editorial Oct 2026 9 min read

Google released the February 2026 Discover core update on 5 February 2026, a broad update to the systems that surface articles in Discover. It launched for English language users in the US, with Google stating it will expand to all countries and languages in the months ahead, without giving specific dates. Google named three changes: more locally relevant content from websites based in the user's country, less sensational content and clickbait, and more in-depth, original and timely content from websites with expertise in a given area. Expertise is identified topic by topic, so a sustained section can earn it while a one-off article usually will not. Google said fluctuations are expected and pointed to its general core update guidance and Get on Discover help page.

What Was the February 2026 Discover Core Update?

The February 2026 Discover core update was a broad update to the systems that surface articles in Google Discover, announced by Google on 5 February 2026. Google said its testing showed people find the Discover experience more useful and worthwhile with the update. It was released to English language users in the US first, with Google stating it would expand to all countries and languages in the months ahead.

A core update specific to Discover is unusual. Most core update coverage is about Search rankings, and Discover has historically been treated as an unpredictable bonus channel. This update spelled out, in Google's own words, what Discover now favours, which makes it one of the more actionable algorithm announcements of 2026 for publishers and content-led brands.

What Did the Discover Core Update Change in 2026?

Google named three improvements, and each one tells a publisher something specific about what to produce. The update shows users more locally relevant content from websites based in their country, reduces sensational content and clickbait, and shows more in-depth, original and timely content from websites with expertise in a given area.

Google's stated changeWhat it rewardsWhat it puts at risk
"Showing users more locally relevant content from websites based in their country"Sites based in the reader's country writing for that marketGeneric content written for everywhere and nowhere
"Reducing sensational content and clickbait in Discover"Accurate headlines that match the articleCuriosity-gap headlines and exaggerated claims
"Showing more in-depth, original, and timely content from websites with expertise in a given area"Depth and original reporting on topics the site knowsThin, rewritten or off-topic coverage

Google also said it will continue to show content personalised to people's creator and source preferences, so the update changes which content is eligible to be favoured rather than replacing personalisation.

Why Does Topic-by-Topic Expertise Matter in 2026?

Because Google said its systems now judge expertise per topic, not per site. The announcement states that "our systems are designed to identify expertise on a topic-by-topic basis," so a site with expertise in several areas or deep focus on one has equal opportunity to appear in Discover. That is a different model from treating a domain as uniformly authoritative.

Google's own example makes it concrete. A local news site with a dedicated gardening section "could have established expertise in gardening, even though it covers other topics." In contrast, "a movie review site that wrote a single article about gardening would likely not." For content teams in 2026 the implication is clear: one-off posts outside your established topics are unlikely to be the ones Discover favours, while sustained, organised coverage of a topic is how expertise is recognised.

  • Build sections, not scattered posts. A dedicated, consistently updated section signals topic expertise.
  • Go deep where you already are. Expanding an existing topic is more credible than chasing a trending one.
  • Be wary of off-topic traffic plays. A single article outside your expertise is Google's own example of what likely will not qualify.

What Does This Mean for Indian Publishers and Brands in 2026?

The local relevance change is the line that matters most for India. Google says the update shows more locally relevant content from websites based in the user's country. Once the update reaches India, as Google said it would expand to all countries and languages, Indian sites writing for Indian readers are positioned to benefit, while foreign sites writing generically about Indian topics may lose ground.

One caution belongs here. Google's announcement gave the US English release and a plan to expand in the months ahead. It did not publish a specific date for India or for individual languages. Any claim that the update has "rolled out in India on" a particular day needs a Google source; the February announcement does not provide one.

The headline test for 2026

Google said the update reduces sensational content and clickbait. A practical check: if the article does not deliver what the headline implies in its first few paragraphs, the headline is a liability in Discover. Write the headline you can prove.

How Should Teams Respond to a Discover Traffic Change in 2026?

Diagnose before reacting, because Google itself says fluctuations are expected and many sites will see no change. Use the Discover performance report in Search Console to see which pages and topics moved, then compare them against Google's three stated priorities.

  1. Segment by topic: group Discover pages by section to see whether losses cluster in topics where you have little depth.
  2. Review headlines: check pages that lost the most for sensational or curiosity-gap headlines.
  3. Check originality: rewritten or aggregated coverage is what the update deprioritises.
  4. Check locality: is the content written for the country your readers are in?
  5. Strengthen sections: consolidate scattered posts into organised topic sections.
  6. Avoid knee-jerk deletions: Google's core update guidance generally favours improving content over removing it.

If you are also working on AI visibility, the Search Console generative AI reports now include a Discover report for generative AI features, which is worth checking alongside the main Discover report.

What Did Google Not Say About the Update in 2026?

The announcement is short, and filling its gaps with guesses is how bad advice spreads. Google did not publish a list of ranking factors, did not give a completion date for the US rollout in the post, did not name a date for any other country or language, and did not give numbers for how much traffic shifted. It pointed site owners to existing guidance instead.

  • No per-country or per-language rollout dates.
  • No traffic impact figures.
  • No new technical requirements for Discover eligibility.
  • No statement that any specific format, such as video or web stories, is favoured.

What Are the Common Mistakes in 2026?

  • Assuming it hit India on the same day as the US. Google gave no India date.
  • Chasing trending topics outside your expertise. Google's own example says that likely will not work.
  • Keeping clickbait headlines. Reducing sensational content is a named goal.
  • Publishing rewritten news. The update favours original, in-depth coverage.
  • Deleting content in a panic. Diagnose by topic first.
  • Treating Discover traffic as guaranteed. Google says fluctuations are normal with core updates.

Key Takeaways for 2026

The February 2026 Discover core update told publishers exactly what Discover wants: local, original, in-depth content from sites with real expertise in the topic.

  • Announced 5 February 2026, first for English language users in the US.
  • Expansion to all countries and languages was promised for the months ahead, with no specific dates.
  • Three named changes: more local content, less clickbait, more in-depth and original expert content.
  • Expertise is judged topic by topic, not site-wide.
  • Diagnose Discover changes by topic and headline before changing anything.
