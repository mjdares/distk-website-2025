Search Console Platform Properties in 2026: Tracking Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube in Google Search

Social posts and videos now rank in Google results, and since July 2026 Search Console can show you exactly which ones and what people searched to find them. Here is how to set it up, read it, and use it to plan content.

Distk Editorial Oct 2026 11 min read

Platform properties are a Search Console property type, announced on 7 July 2026 and globally available from 29 July 2026, that report how Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube content performs on Google Search, Discover and Google News. Each account is added and verified as its own property, data takes a few days to appear, and ownership is periodically re-checked, pausing access if a login expires. Three reports are available: Performance for clicks, impressions, CTR and position by post and query; Insights for trends, top content and query groups; and Achievements for click milestones over the last 28 days. Google's guide recommends query themes, the 24-hour filter, comparison mode for shorts versus full videos, and annotations for caption changes. Platform properties do not track views on the platform itself, and only four platforms are supported.

What Are Platform Properties in Search Console in 2026?

Platform properties are a Search Console property type that shows how your Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube content performs on Google Search. Google announced them on 7 July 2026 and made them globally available to everyone on 29 July 2026. Instead of a website, you verify a social account or channel, and Search Console reports the clicks, impressions and search terms that brought people to your posts from Google Search, Discover and Google News.

Google frames the purpose plainly: content creators and publishers use many channels beyond their own websites, and people increasingly look for firsthand perspectives and different content formats. In 2026 a brand's Instagram reel or YouTube tutorial can rank in Google results alongside its website, and until July there was no first-party way to see that traffic at all. Platform properties close that gap, including for creators who have no website.

Why Do Social Accounts Need Search Data in 2026?

Because Google is now a meaningful discovery channel for social content, and the words people search with are the most honest brief a content team can get. A creator who knows that a specific search term suddenly started sending people to last week's video can make the next video, caption or hashtag around that term. Google's own guide makes exactly this point: search themes show what your audience cares about.

For brands in 2026 this changes three conversations. Social teams get evidence beyond platform likes. SEO teams get visibility into a channel that was previously invisible to them. And leadership gets a single place to see whether a YouTube channel or Instagram account is pulling people in from Google, which is often the question behind "should we keep investing in this account".

  • Creators without websites get Search Console data for the first time.
  • Brand social teams can see which posts rank in Google, not just which posts trend on the platform.
  • SEO teams can include social content in search reporting instead of treating it as a separate world.
  • Agencies can show clients a joined-up view of search visibility across site and social.

How Do You Set Up a Platform Property in 2026?

You add each account as its own property and verify ownership by authorising the connection. Google's help centre describes the steps: go to the Search Console welcome screen to add a property, or open the property selector dropdown in the sidebar and click Add property, then click Add next to the platform you want and follow the prompts. Once ownership is verified, you see a confirmation and click Go to property.

  1. Open Search Console.
  2. Use the welcome screen, or open the property selector dropdown and click Add property.
  3. Choose one of the four platforms: Instagram, TikTok, X or YouTube.
  4. Follow the onscreen verification steps to authorise the connection.
  5. After the confirmation message, click Go to property.
  6. Wait a few days for data to appear.
  7. Repeat for every account and every platform you want to track.
The shortcut most people miss in 2026

Google's analysis guide notes that if you already claimed your Search profile, all of your verified accounts are automatically added as properties in Search Console. Check the property selector before verifying anything manually.

One operational detail matters for agencies managing client accounts. Google says ownership is periodically checked for security, and if the connection is lost, for example because an external login expired, access to the platform property pauses until you re-verify. When you re-verify you get the same reports back without waiting for data to accumulate again. Put re-verification on a checklist, because a paused property looks like a traffic drop to anyone who does not know why.

What Do the Three Platform Property Reports Show in 2026?

The Performance report is the detailed view, Insights is the summary, and Achievements tracks milestones. Together they show clicks, impressions, average click-through rate and average position for the account's content in Google, the queries and posts driving that traffic, and where your audience is. Insights and Performance default to the last 28 days.

One nuance in the Insights page is worth knowing before you report numbers. Google's help centre says the top summary card shows all clicks to your property across Google, including web, image, video and news searches, while the detailed lists below focus on web search results. That is why the cards below can add up to less than the summary card. It is not a data error.

How Should Teams Analyse Social Content Performance in 2026?

Google published a full guide on analysing social and video content, and it is unusually practical. The techniques below come straight from that guide and turn platform properties from a passive dashboard into a content planning tool.

  • Read query themes. In the Insights report, the query groups card shows top, trending up and trending down groups. Use the rising groups to plan the next video, caption or hashtag.
  • Catch spikes with the 24-hour filter. In the Performance report, the 24 hours filter shows whether a new post is suddenly drawing search traffic, so you can cross-promote it while interest is live.
  • Compare formats with comparison mode. Google suggests comparing YouTube URLs containing /watch against /shorts/, and Instagram URLs containing /p/ against /reels/.
  • Compare playlists with a page filter. Filter on the word playlist to compare playlist pages, noting this shows the playlist page itself, not the videos inside it.
  • Compare across platforms by exporting. Export each property and combine them in one spreadsheet to see how the same content performs on YouTube versus TikTok.
  • Annotate caption and title changes. Add a Search Console annotation on the date you rewrite a YouTube title or TikTok caption, then watch whether search performance changes.
  • Revive older content. Look for older posts regaining traction and consider pinning them, mentioning them elsewhere or making a part two.

What Do Platform Properties Not Report in 2026?

They report Google, not the platform, and the help centre says so directly: platform properties only show how your content performs on Google Search, and they do not track when people see your content on the platform itself. For anyone tempted to replace platform analytics with Search Console, that boundary is the whole point.

  • No on-platform views. It will not show how many times your video appeared on TikTok.
  • Four platforms only. Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube. Other networks are not listed.
  • One property per account. Multiple profiles on one platform each need their own property.
  • Recent setups show partial data. Charts start from when collection began.
  • Clicks count even inside Google's viewer. Google notes a click is recorded even if it opens the video inside the Google viewer, so a click is not always a visit to the platform.
  • No conversions. Like all Search Console data, it stops at the click.

What Are the Common Mistakes in 2026?

  • Expecting on-platform metrics. Views and likes on the platform are not reported.
  • Verifying only the main account. Every profile and every platform needs its own property.
  • Missing the Search profile shortcut. Claimed Search profiles add verified accounts automatically.
  • Panicking at a paused property. An expired login pauses access; re-verify and the history returns.
  • Judging a property in week one. Data takes a few days to appear.
  • Adding up Insights cards to match the summary. The summary includes image, video and news clicks; the cards focus on web.
  • Changing captions without an annotation. You lose the before and after.

Search Console Platform Properties: FAQs

What are platform properties in Search Console?

A property type that shows how your Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube content performs on Google Search, Discover and Google News. Google announced them on 7 July 2026 and made them globally available on 29 July 2026.

Which social platforms does Search Console support?

Four: Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube. Each account or channel must be added as its own property.

Do platform properties show TikTok or Instagram views?

No. Google states platform properties only show how your content performs on Google Search, not when people see your content on the platform itself.

How long until data appears after setup?

Google says it takes a few days for data to appear. Recently created properties show only partial data from when collection started.

Why did my platform property stop working?

Ownership is periodically checked. If the connection is lost, for example because an external login expired, access pauses until you re-verify. Re-verifying restores the same reports without waiting for data to rebuild.

Do I need a website to use platform properties?

No. Google built platform properties to help site owners and creators, even those without their own website, see how their social and video content is discovered on Search.
